Hachikō (ハチ公)

1923 – 1935

The Dog Who Never Forgot

Hachikō was a Japanese Akita dog remembered for his extraordinary loyalty to his owner, Professor Hidesaburō Ueno. After Ueno died unexpectedly in 1925, Hachikō continued returning to Shibuya Station every day, waiting for his owner to come back. For nearly ten years, he returned to the station, becoming a symbol of loyalty and devotion in Japan. His story eventually became known around the world.

Biographies

  • Hachikō (ハチ公) was a Japanese Akita dog born on November 10, 1923, in Ōdate, Akita Prefecture. In 1924, he was brought to Tokyo and became the companion of Hidesaburō Ueno, a professor at Tokyo Imperial University.
  • After Professor Ueno died suddenly in 1925, Hachikō continued returning to Shibuya Station, where he had regularly met his owner. For nearly a decade, he waited at the station, becoming widely known for his extraordinary loyalty and devotion.
  • Hachikō died on March 8, 1935. His story became a lasting symbol of loyalty in Japan, and a bronze statue was later erected in his memory at Shibuya Station.
